Investor pitch

Invest in the public-records intelligence layer for Florida real estate.

Florida property records are public, but the workflow is fragmented. Investors, attorneys, contractors, title researchers, and lenders waste hours checking separate clerk websites, auction platforms, property appraiser databases, permit portals, GIS tools, tax collector pages, and PDFs.

DeedScout turns that fragmented workflow into one searchable intelligence layer.

We are starting with Florida tax deed investors, then expanding into parcel research, permit intelligence, surplus tracking, contractor opportunity signals, and county-level market intelligence.

County data is free. The workflow is expensive.

DeedScout does not sell public records. It sells speed, organization, monitoring, alerts, filtering, exports, and research workflows.

DeedScout is a Florida property-intelligence platform built from public records.

Today, real estate investors, tax deed buyers, contractors, title researchers, and local service businesses have to jump between county clerk sites, property appraiser databases, auction platforms, permit portals, GIS systems, and PDFs just to answer one question: "Is this property worth researching?"

DeedScout brings those scattered records into one workflow.

The first wedge is Florida tax deed research. We organize all 67 Florida counties, link directly to official clerk and auction sources, track upcoming sales, support parcel research, and build watchlists and alerts around county activity. From there, we layer in parcel lookup, permit history, subdivision data, plat activity, surplus-funds tracking, AVM, skip trace, and contractor opportunity signals.

The long-term vision is to become the public-records intelligence layer for Florida real estate.

The business model is simple: free public search to build traffic, Pro subscriptions for watchlists, alerts, AVM, skip tracing, CSV exports, and parcel notebooks, and higher-tier team/API access for investor groups, law firms, lenders, and service companies.

Investor FAQ

How does DeedScout make money?

Freemium model: free tools build traffic and trust. Paid subscriptions unlock watchlists, alerts, exports, AVM, skip tracing, saved research, team workspaces, and advanced county monitoring.

What is the first market?

Florida tax deed investors and property researchers.

Can this expand beyond Florida?

Yes. Florida is the first state because of active public records, strong investor demand, and fragmented county systems. The same model can expand to other high-activity real estate states.

What is the moat?

The workflow layer: county coverage, source mapping, data freshness, user watchlists, saved research, historical activity, permit signals, parcel enrichment, alerts, and investor-specific workflows.
