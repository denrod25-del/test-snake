Florida Water Quality

What's actually in your Florida tap water.

EPA's drinking-water data is public but scattered across systems that use different units, different contaminant names, and different definitions of "detected." This normalizes it: one utility, one page, every number traceable to its source.

Find your water utility

A ZIP code is a mail route, not a water service area — several utilities often share one. You'll get a ranked list, not a single answer. The definitive source is the utility name printed on your water bill.

What gets measured, and against what limit

The contaminant dictionary this API normalizes everything onto — every limit names the authority that set it. Contaminants with an enforceable limit are listed first. A contaminant with no federal limit is still measured and reported: no limit means EPA has not set one, not that any amount is known to be safe.

Known limitations
ZIP codes are not service-area boundaries. SDWIS inventory is self-reported by utilities and lags by a reporting cycle. Sample results reflect the monitoring EPA requires, which is periodic — not a continuous measurement of what is in your tap right now.
Verification required
This is aggregated public data for research. It is not a substitute for your utility's own Consumer Confidence Report, and not a substitute for a certified lab test of water at your tap. Nothing here is health advice.

Full method notes and per-source freshness: Data Sources · System Status. If you're on a private well, none of this applies — well water is unregulated and untested unless you test it.
